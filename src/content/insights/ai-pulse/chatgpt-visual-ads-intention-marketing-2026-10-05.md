On 5 October 2026, OpenAI announced plans to test visual advertisements alongside image generation in ChatGPT. The initial US test is scheduled for later in October. For marketers, the interesting question is how to be useful when a person is exploring a possibility before they have formed a conventional product search.

## What happened

OpenAI said the first test would involve users on its Free and Go plans and an initial group of advertisers. The company said the ads would be clearly labelled, remain separate from the generated image and would not influence ChatGPT's answers. These are the announced design and policy commitments, rather than results from a broadly available rollout.

The distinction between an advertisement beside an answer and the answer itself is fundamental. Paying for visibility should not be described as buying an assistant's recommendation. A marketing team needs to understand the placement it is evaluating and what claims the platform actually makes about it.

## Why it matters

Consider a hypothetical customer exploring how a small living room might look with a different layout. The person may be thinking about space, light and comfort rather than searching for a particular sofa. A relevant advertisement could introduce an option at that moment, but relevance would depend on more than an attractive product image.

The offer needs to fit the practical problem. Dimensions, availability, delivery constraints and a clear route to compare alternatives may matter more than a broad aspirational message. If the advertisement creates an expectation that the product page cannot support, the handover wastes the opportunity and can weaken trust.

For the marketer, this suggests preparing the information around an offer as carefully as the creative. The destination should help a person move from an imagined outcome to a realistic choice. It should be clear which features are demonstrated, which are illustrative and which depend on a customer's own circumstances.

## The bigger shift

AI interfaces may bring advertising closer to the formation of an intention. That is an interpretation of the opportunity, not proof that every exploratory conversation represents a purchase. A person can investigate an idea for entertainment, research or a future project with no immediate commercial need.

Measurement should preserve that uncertainty. Clicks and visits can show interest, but they do not establish that an advertisement created a sale that would otherwise not have happened. A sensible test needs a defined audience, a useful commercial outcome and a comparison method proportionate to the budget and available data.

This connects to [putting the marketing objective before the AI tool](/insights/ai-strategy/why-ai-strategy-beats-ai-tools/). A new placement is worth considering when it helps address a known customer decision. It does not automatically justify diverting budget from a channel whose contribution the business already understands.

## My take

Treat paid visibility and earned confidence as separate responsibilities. An ad can make an offer visible. Product information, credible evidence and the subsequent experience must make the offer worth considering.

Before joining a new advertising test, write down the customer situation the campaign is meant to serve. Specify the useful next action and the evidence required to judge it. Review the destination with somebody unfamiliar with the offer: can they understand what is available, what it costs and whether it fits their need without filling in missing details?

Then test within a bounded budget and resist drawing conclusions from novelty alone. The opportunity is to meet a person with relevant information at a useful moment. The lasting advantage will belong to businesses that can turn that moment into an honest, coherent customer experience, rather than merely occupying another rectangle on a screen.
