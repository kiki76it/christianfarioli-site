An AI leadership offsite should help executives agree where AI might serve business priorities, what the organisation can realistically support and who owns the next decision. Participants should leave with a small number of recorded choices, the evidence still needed and accountable owners.

For an offsite planning for 2027, use the year as a planning horizon. Decisions can draw on evidence available now while making assumptions about future capabilities, costs and adoption explicit. No one needs to pretend to know which products or market conditions will exist throughout the following year.

A demonstration can help explain a capability. It does not establish that your data, processes or people are ready to use it. I would design the session around decisions the leadership team can actually make.

## Choose the decisions that belong in the room

Begin with one question that matters to the business. It might be whether to investigate an AI-assisted customer service workflow, invest in staff capability or resolve fragmented ownership across existing projects. Avoid placing every possible AI application on the agenda.

Ask the executive sponsor which decisions are available: approve a discovery exercise, allocate staff time, choose between two priorities, or defer an initiative pending evidence. Confirm the limits of the room's authority. A team can recommend an investment without being authorised to commit the budget.

The [UK Government's AI Playbook](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government/artificial-intelligence-playbook-for-the-uk-government-html#identifying-use-cases-for-ai) bases use-case selection on business and user needs. It is public-sector guidance. My recommendation for a corporate offsite is to bring a clearly described operational problem before considering a particular AI approach.

For an AI leadership offsite in Dubai, the venue and international guest list may shape logistics, but the business question should shape the session. Agree the working language, level of technical detail and confidentiality arrangements with the organiser in advance.

## Prepare evidence that executives can challenge

I would circulate a short pre-read, perhaps two pages with supporting material available separately. Explain the decision sought and describe no more than two or three candidate opportunities. For each, include the current process, the people affected, available data and the proposed benefit.

Distinguish observed facts from estimates and assumptions. A measured backlog is evidence of a current problem. A claim that AI will halve it is a proposition requiring a test. Label supplier statements as supplier statements and identify who has checked them.

Finance should clarify the cost categories that matter. Operations should explain the workflow and its exceptions. Technology and data owners should identify access, integration and information-quality constraints. HR should bring questions about skills, responsibilities and how affected staff will be involved.

Ask each participant to submit one concern and one question before the meeting. The facilitator can group these without attributing every comment, where appropriate. This helps expose disagreement before the most senior voice establishes the direction.

Use approved, sanitised material if the session includes an external facilitator or a tool demonstration. Decide what may be shared, retained or recorded. The event invitation should not become permission to upload confidential business information into a presentation service.

## Match the format to the intended work

A keynote can establish shared concepts and give leaders questions to consider. It suits an audience that needs a common starting point. Reserve discussion time separately if the organisation also expects a decision; audience questions alone may not resolve competing priorities.

A facilitated discussion suits a leadership team that has evidence to examine and choices to make. The facilitator should keep the group on the decision, surface disagreement and distinguish a conclusion from an unresolved assumption. The executive sponsor retains decision authority.

A workshop asks participants to produce something: a proposed pilot scope, a map of responsibilities or a draft business case. It requires prepared inputs and enough time for participants to work on the artefact, receive challenge and revise it.

These formats can be combined, but the time must add up. A keynote added to a full working agenda needs its own allocation. The [guide to choosing an AI keynote speaker in Dubai](/insights/ai-strategy/how-to-choose-an-ai-keynote-speaker-in-dubai/) covers speaker selection; the agenda below concerns how the leadership team uses the meeting.

## An example 90-minute executive agenda

This is a proposed working session for a small, prepared leadership group, not a mandatory programme or a guaranteed result. It assumes participants have read the brief and that the sponsor can make or formally recommend the decisions in scope.

The six blocks total 90 minutes. The opening includes a short common briefing; there is no additional keynote or break hidden outside the allocation.

## Test assumptions about 2027 without forecasting certainty

Ask which parts of the proposal depend on conditions that might change. Would it still make sense if implementation took longer, review required more staff time or the preferred supplier changed its terms? These are planning scenarios, not predictions.

Consider a hypothetical leadership team evaluating AI assistance for customer enquiries. A compelling demonstration might show a useful draft response. The executive discussion should examine whether approved answers exist, who checks exceptions and how a customer reaches a person when the suggestion is inadequate.

The team might choose a limited internal test rather than customer-facing execution. It could ask Operations to establish the current workload, IT to confirm the permitted environment and the service manager to define acceptable responses. That is a useful decision even if the offsite does not approve a deployment.

Also test the people assumptions. If managers inherit additional review duties, identify what they can stop doing and what training they need. The discussion of [AI and middle management](/insights/future-of-work/ai-middle-management/) explores how information preparation and managerial responsibility may change together.

## Leave with a record that someone can act on

Give one person responsibility for capturing decisions during the session. Record the action, its rationale, the accountable owner, any required approval, the evidence due and the next review point. Keep outstanding objections visible, especially when a sponsor decides to proceed despite them.

Write the action precisely. “Explore AI in customer service” leaves too much open. “The Operations Director will return with a scoped internal test, data permissions and an estimated supervision requirement for the next executive review” defines work that can be checked.

Read the record back in the final ten minutes. Confirm that named owners accept their responsibilities and have the authority to obtain the inputs. Circulate the agreed version afterwards and place the follow-up on an existing management agenda.

## Recognise what the offsite cannot settle

Ninety minutes will not validate a complex system, resolve every data question or establish an organisation-wide AI strategy. A larger group, substantial disagreement or missing evidence may require more time. Consequential decisions may need specialist review outside the session.

If participants arrive without a usable brief, change the objective to identifying what must be investigated. Avoid recording enthusiasm as investment approval. An explicit decision to defer, with a reason and owner, is more useful than an unsupported commitment.

Before booking the session, prepare a one-page organiser's brief: the decision sought, the people who need to attend, the evidence available and the work you want to leave with. That gives the sponsor, speaker and facilitator a concrete basis for shaping the offsite.
