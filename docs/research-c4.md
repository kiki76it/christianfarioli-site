## Practical contribution and boundaries

The communication and listening plan uses narrative phases rather than another matrix:

1. Before the announcement: agree the purpose, scope, open decisions and what managers can truthfully say.
2. Before designing the trial: involve staff with different experiences, invite concrete concerns and explain what can actually change.
3. Before first use: explain data purposes, records, employee assessment, support and participation expectations.
4. During the trial: offer different feedback routes, distinguish incidents from suggestions and respond visibly.
5. At review: report changes made, outstanding issues and reasons; examine trust separately from activity.

There are three clearly labelled example manager messages and two groups of example listening questions. Messages are explicitly conditional on the organisation having made the stated decisions. The final follow-up describes a hypothetical action rather than pretending that a real employer has taken it.

The article avoids promises of permanent job security, treats objections as possible sources of information, rejects undisclosed repurposing of monitoring records and avoids promising anonymity without suitable arrangements. It distinguishes mandatory work participation from endorsement, and tool usage from trust or consent to additional data purposes. Privacy and employee-assessment arrangements are referred to the appropriate organisational specialists; there are no assertions about a particular jurisdiction's legal obligations.
